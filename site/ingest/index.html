01 / Workspaces

Different shoots.
Your way of working.

You photograph more than one kind of thing. Save a workspace for each: how you browse and review, where files go, how they’re named, which metadata they receive, and what happens after import.

A builder that walks you through it.

Choose a starting point or begin with a basic setup. The guided Workspace Builder takes you through the decisions one at a time, with examples as you go. Prefer to set it up yourself? Edit the same sections directly.

Start with a destination and keep your original filenames. Add questions, photo groups, metadata, and actions when you need them. Review the result, save it, and choose that workspace for your next shoot.

03 / Command-K

Know what you want to do?
Type it.

Keep your hands on the keyboard without memorizing a shortcut for every action. Press ⌘K, search in your own words, and run the command. Ingest shows shortcuts and explains when an action needs a different view or selection.

  • “review”

    Inspect a set of photos.

  • “sidebars”

    Clear space around the photo.

  • “focus peaking”

    Inspect fine detail.

  • “closed eyes”

    Find photos flagged for a closer look.

  • “subfolders”

    Look through nested folders.

These examples describe the Mac app’s palette. The website’s Search button searches this guide.

04 / Review

Find the expression.
Check the detail.
Keep the photograph.

Move quickly through RAW previews, then take a closer look where it matters. Review brings your photographs, face close-ups, and focus inspection into one place.

Every face deserves a look.

Cycle through detected faces with [ and ]. Inspect expressions, check eyes, and use focus peaking to examine detail. Uncertain assessments stay visible.

Compare the contenders.

Keep a reference while you inspect candidates. Tag keepers, set stars and color labels, or mark photos reviewed. Press F in Browse to hide surrounding controls and focus on the photo.

Make a big shoot manageable.

Keep bursts, similar frames, and exposure brackets together. Built-in face and sharpness analysis runs on your Mac and helps direct your attention. It does not choose your keepers for you.

05 / Metadata Assist

More typing.
Less hunting through fields.

Add the details that make a photograph useful later. Type to find a field, enter its value, and review the changes before applying them to one photo or a selection.

A place you can search for.

  1. Open Metadata Assist and type ;loc.
  2. Choose Map Location and press Return.
  3. Search an address or place through Apple Maps, then review the map pin before applying.

Map Location writes GPS coordinates. Descriptive IPTC place names are separate. Compatible apps can read the saved metadata; Apple Photos may need a JPEG export with metadata included when a RAW sidecar is not read.

Explore metadata workflows →

Keywords you’ve already used.

Suggestions draw from keywords Ingest has encountered, so familiar names and subjects are ready to complete. Keywords append by default; review the operation before applying.

Professional details, ready to reuse.

Save copyright, credit, captions, and other IPTC details in metadata templates. Use workspace answers in templates and apply them to verified imported copies.

Less repeated typing.

Caption codes expand short codes into known text. Copy selected metadata fields between photos, and reuse the same venue or event information across a job.

06 / Import and handoff

Off the card.
Verified. Ready for what’s next.

Review which photos will be copied, where they’ll go, and what they’ll be called. Ingest verifies the copies, then runs the next steps you’ve chosen.

A second copy, checked too.

Add a second destination for another verified copy. Use a separate physical drive for a meaningful backup. Card cleanup requires successful verification at every required destination; leaving the card alone is the default.

Import everything. Open only the keepers.

Choose photos separately for each after-import action. Import the whole shoot while opening only tagged copies in Lightroom. Other actions can reveal files in Finder, send them to Apple Photos, or run a Shortcut or script.

Carry your work into your editor.

Supported stars, color labels, and metadata are saved in image files or XMP sidecars for compatible editing apps. Ingest’s on/off tags and review state stay separate from those shared fields.

07 / Existing folders

Start with the photos
you already have.

Open a folder on your Mac or a connected drive. Browse, review, and organize its photographs without first copying everything into a new library.

Find the photograph buried one folder deeper.

Turn on Subfolders to see photos beneath the current folder. Select a photograph to identify its containing folder, and use favorites and saved filters to return to useful views.

Make the archive make sense.

Organize existing files by details such as date, camera, lens, or keywords. Build the structure, inspect the filename and destination previews, and review changes before applying Copy, Move, or Rename.

RAW and JPEG companions can stay together as one photograph while you browse and work.

08 / The details matter

Built for the way
photographers look.

Useful tools for the moments when the thumbnail doesn’t tell the whole story.

Look beyond the camera preview.

Compare a rendered RAW image with the camera JPEG, with linked zoom and pan. Use the sensor-based RAW histogram to inspect highlight headroom and clipping before white balance and rendering change the picture.

The tone histogram describes the displayed image. Choose the sensor histogram for supported RAW files when you need to inspect the recorded exposure.

RAW inspection explained →

Your client sent their favorites.
Find them together.

Paste a list of filenames or image numbers into Select from Pick List. Ingest matches them to the current photos and identifies missing or ambiguous entries before you select the matches.

Get multiple cameras back in order.

Adjust capture times when camera clocks were out of sync, so photographs from the same event sort into the right sequence.

Your Fujifilm settings, right there.

See the detected film simulation and available camera settings in a supported Fujifilm JPEG, including JPEG companions in RAW + JPEG pairs. Copy or export recipe text, save notes, or add the simulation as a keyword.

Recorded settings and your notes, not a guessed custom recipe name or a profile to load onto the camera.

Apple Photos, when you want it.

Browse your Photos library, review photos, and send selected images to albums. Turn integration off for a professional workspace and on for personal photography, with the global integration setting enabled.

09 / Send to Decks

Your keepers.
Ready to be seen.

Send selected still photographs to Decks for layouts, frames, and social presentation. Choose original files or prepared JPEG copies, then start a new project or add to an existing one in Decks.

Use Decks on your Mac, or transfer through iCloud or the system sharing options to a compatible Decks app. Your Ingest originals stay in place.
