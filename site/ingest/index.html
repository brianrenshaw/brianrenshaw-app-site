The command bar

Find and run commands with ⌘K.

Type a few letters to find a command, favorite folder, preset, or workspace. Use the arrow keys to select a result and Return to open or run it.

Each command shows its keyboard shortcut. You can pin frequently used commands to the top of the list.

Import

Import photos with names and metadata.

Choose which photos to import and where to save them. Preview their filenames and select a metadata preset before copying. Ingest verifies each copy against its source, then applies names and metadata.

Save your destinations and naming preferences so they are ready for the next shoot.

A closer look at importing →

Cull

Compare, rate, and select photos.

Browse quickly, compare similar frames side by side, and make your selections with the keyboard. Tag, reject, rate, and label as you go.

Zoom and pan stay synchronized between photos in Compare view. Filter by tags or ratings to review your selections.

No automatic rating or rejection. Every tag, rating, and label is one you applied.

A closer look at culling →

Manage

Organize photos in existing folders.

Open a folder on your Mac to browse and manage its photos. Rename files, add keywords and captions, and organize photos with ratings and labels.

Return to favorite folders, save filtered views, and switch workspaces for different kinds of shoots. Find them by name in the command bar.

About Ingest

I built Ingest because I wanted a modern Mac app for the part of the workflow Photo Mechanic has covered for years. Importing, culling, renaming, and getting to the edit. Quick previews for Sony ARW and Fujifilm RAF, a command bar, and a build for Apple silicon.

There’s a place for AI culling, and the tools built for it are good at it. It just isn’t what everyone needs. Ingest assumes you’d rather make the selects yourself, and it’s built to keep up with you.

  • You make the selects. Nothing rates or rejects on your behalf.
  • Copies checked against the source. How verification works →
  • Card metadata and filenames stay untouched. How cards are protected →
  • Your photos stay on your Mac. No account or analytics. Read the privacy details →
