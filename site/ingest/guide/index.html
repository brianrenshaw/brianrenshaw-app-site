Ingest / The guide

Import, cull, and manage your photos.

Import a new shoot, choose your keepers, or organize photos already in your folders. This guide covers the tools for each job, with the command bar connecting them. Start with the quick start for your first import, or jump to the part you need below.

Cards and folders

Put a card in and it appears in the sources list with its name and free space, and Ingest opens it. Press ⌘O to open an ordinary folder instead. Either way you are looking at photographs within a second, before the app has finished reading everything.

The sidebar keeps folder search and your Mac’s folder tree prominent. Selecting a folder opens it immediately. Favorites, Recent, and Saved Views have collapsible sections. ⌘D adds the current folder to Favorites. Show photos in subfolders is visible beside the folder name and defaults off; when enabled, selecting a photo identifies its containing folder. Finder’s Open With Ingest opens the photo’s folder and selects it.

⌘[ and ⌘] go back and forward through what you have opened, like a browser, so switching to the card and back to yesterday's folder costs one keystroke.

⌘E ejects. Ingest closes every file it has open on the card first, which is why eject does not fail with the usual complaint about a file in use. If a card disappears, Ingest closes the source and cancels an active import. Verified copies are retained; check the result for incomplete groups.

There is also a menu bar item, on by default, showing the progress of a running ingest, the cards that are in, and your recent folders. It is there so a copy can run while you work in another app.

Apple Photos, inside Ingest

Choose Apple Photos in the source sidebar and connect to your System Photo Library. Ingest requests Photos read/write access when you connect or send photos, not simply when it launches. It uses the library designated as the System Photo Library in Photos; it does not open an arbitrary library file.

Integration is on by default. Turn off Settings → General → Apple Photos integration to hide the Photos source, commands, send controls, and Photos actions. Your saved Photos review state and recipes remain available if you turn it back on. Finish an active Photos operation or import review before disabling it.

Use Library, Favorites, or an ordinary album to browse. Find albums narrows the sidebar while keeping matching folders and their parents. Search photos by filename, caption, or keywords; caption and keyword search requires macOS 27. Unfiled shows photos outside ordinary albums. Unreviewed tracks what you have reviewed in Ingest, independently of album membership.

The first search across a large library can take time as Photos supplies filenames and keywords. Your current photos stay visible while results are prepared.

Review and file a selection

Apple Photos uses the same browsing layout as files and folders. Choose Grid, Preview, or Split from the photo controls, or press ⌘1, ⌘2, or ⌘3. Preview includes a selection filmstrip; Split keeps the grid beside the preview. Space opens Preview and returns to the previous layout.

Click once to select a photo, use the arrow keys to move through the current view, and use Command-click or Shift-click to select several photos. Available image detail appears progressively; recently viewed previews are reused.

Photos stored on your Mac work without internet. If an older photo’s larger image is only in iCloud, Apple Photos must download it; a local thumbnail may appear first. That wait depends on your internet connection and Photos, not Ingest. For offline viewing, choose Download Originals to this Mac in Photos → Settings → iCloud and let syncing finish. This uses more storage. The info button beside the album title and Ingest Settings → General explain this limitation in the app.

The inspector shows basic photo information first, then loads expanded Histogram and Camera Details sections as needed. On connection, you can begin browsing while Ingest prepares your albums. Unfiled and album organization become available when that preparation finishes.

Use the toolbar’s Sources and Inspector buttons, ⌥⌘S and ⌘I, to show or hide the sidebars. The right-hand inspector follows Settings → Sidebar information and includes tags, ratings, color labels, favorites, Photo Info, and album/review actions. Its histogram measures the current rendered Photos image. Choose a photo to see its information; widen the window if the shared layout has collapsed a pane to make room.

Select photos, then press G to add them to one or more albums. Press R to mark them reviewed, ⇧R to mark and advance, F to toggle favorite, or Space to preview. Ingest tags, color labels, and reviewed marks stay local. Favorites are native Photos changes.

Create albums and folders with the plus menu. Use a collection’s context menu to rename or move it. Within an editable album, ⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→ move selected photos earlier or later. Pressing Delete removes photos from that album only. Delete from Library… is a separate action with confirmation; it removes photos from every album and follows Photos’ Recently Deleted and iCloud behavior.

Metadata and organization

Press M to review metadata changes. Favorites, capture dates, and locations work on supported macOS versions. Native captions, keywords, and star ratings require macOS 27. On earlier versions, ratings are local to Ingest; copying those ratings into Photos later requires an explicit review.

Choose Organize Photos into Albums to preview a grouping recipe, folder and album paths, manual assignments, and photo order. Apply adds photos to the reviewed destinations without removing unrelated album contents. Rules run when you apply them; they do not continuously synchronize. Shared Album publishing and native Smart Album rule editing are not included.

Send files to Apple Photos

Select files in a writable folder and choose Send to Apple Photos…. Review the album and import choices first. The default uses existing JPEGs or JPEG companions; files with no JPEG are reported as skipped. Choose RAW or RAW + JPEG explicitly when needed. RAW + JPEG creates separate Photos assets, not a paired asset. Ingest stages copies and verifies their bytes before importing, leaving the source files unchanged.

Memory cards must finish ingest and backups before sending. File-based Organize offers Send Groups to Photos…; a Photos post-ingest action opens the same reviewed flow. Browse JPEG Export can offer sending after export, and Social Export’s completion receipt can send finished JPEGs.

Import receipts reuse confirmed previous Ingest imports by content hash. This is not a scan for every duplicate already in your library. Open Photos Import History to reconcile an uncertain submission. Ingest does not silently retry a submitted import whose result is unknown or missing; Allow a New Import is an intentional retry that may create a duplicate. A successful import confirms addition to Photos, not completion of iCloud upload.

Use Photos in Social Export

Select stills in Photos and choose Import into Social Export or press ⇧⌘E. Ingest keeps a durable local copy of each current rendered image for the project, along with its Photos reference. Cloud-only originals may need downloading through Apple Photos. Those retained project inputs are separate from the thumbnail cache.

Photos imports, album changes, metadata edits, and deletions can sync through iCloud if you have enabled iCloud Photos. See Photos privacy and storage.

Browsing, and why it is fast

Use the Grid, Preview, and Split icons to choose your view. Press I to cycle photo information in any of them. The inspector shows Histogram, Camera Details, Caption, Keywords, and Location by default. Settings → Sidebar Information lets you choose fields and drag them into your preferred order. Metadata shortcuts open the appropriate editor. Grid sizing extends to one photo at a time; arrow keys stay with your photos by default.

A raw file is large and slow to develop. Many cameras also write a finished preview inside that raw file for their own screens. Ingest finds that JPEG and reads only those bytes. A Sony ARW is 76 MB; the preview Ingest reads out of it is a few hundred kilobytes, and the full size JPEG inside it is about six.

Embedded previews keep ordinary browsing fast, with no import step before you can look. If a file has no usable preview or paired image, Ingest can render the selected photo in a separate helper and cache the result. That first fallback preview is slower; whole cards are not rendered in advance.

Arrow keys move, ⇧ extends the selection, and ⌘ with an arrow moves the focus ring without disturbing what is selected. ⌘+ and ⌘- resize the thumbnails, and so does a pinch.

Sorting and filtering

The bar above the photographs carries the same controls in Browse, Apple Photos, Ingest, and Organize. Sort reads its own state, so you can see what the order is without opening anything, and the button beside it turns the order around. The criteria are capture time, filename, rating, color label, date modified, and file size; Apple Photos adds album order, and Organize adds the group order of the recipe you are applying. Each one is worded for what it sorts, so a rating sort offers highest first and lowest first rather than ascending and descending.

Filter opens a panel rather than a menu: show all photographs or only the tagged, unmarked, or untagged ones; a minimum star rating; any combination of color labels; and a kind, such as brackets, singles, video, or new since the last import. Everything you have chosen appears as a row of chips under the bar, each with a cross that clears that one thing, beside a count of how many photographs are showing and a Clear that returns all of them. Saved views live in the same panel, and ⇧⌘S saves the current filter as a new one.

Photographs are ordered by when they were taken, down to the fraction of a second, so two cameras at the same event interleave correctly. A folder remembers the order you left it in.

Which files are supported, and what happens to the rest, is on the support page.

The preview

Space opens the large view, and Space again closes it. ⌘1, ⌘2, and ⌘3 switch between grid, preview, and both at once.

Z goes to 100 per cent at the pointer, so whatever is under the cursor stays under the cursor, and Z again returns to fit. ⌥1, ⌥2, and ⌥4 are fixed zoom levels, and F fits. A pinch, a two finger drag, and a two finger double tap do the same things.

The zoom is sticky as you move between photographs, which is the point: you set 100 per cent on an eye, then arrow through five frames and compare the same eye in each.

In ordinary preview mode, at 100 per cent Ingest shows the full size JPEG from inside the raw file, which for a Sony body is the whole 9504 pixel frame. When a raw file's preview is smaller than its JPEG twin, Ingest uses the twin, and the information overlay tells you which pixels you are actually judging.

Compare and survey

C puts two photographs side by side. N shows up to four. Zoom and pan are locked across the panes, so zooming into a face in one zooms into the same place in the others, which is the only way to judge two nearly identical frames.

Marking keys act on the highlighted pane, so you can tag your choice without leaving the comparison. Esc leaves.

On a bracket set, C opens every frame at once, which is how you check whether the window is blown in all five.

RAW histogram and preview comparison

The inspector’s RAW / JPEG / Compare selector starts on RAW for each RAW photo. Supported Bayer and X-Trans sensor data, including DNG files with these mosaics, is read directly with black levels subtracted. White balance, demosaic, and rendering tone curves do not affect this histogram.

RGB curves remain visible by default. Use Combined / RGB / R / G / B to combine curves or isolate a channel. An individual RAW channel shows its headroom and clipped percentage. Both green photosite populations are combined, and channels are normalized so green does not dominate simply because the sensor contains more green photosites. Use Log (stops) / Linear to change the RAW scale (also available by right-clicking). The default runs from −16 stops to the white level at 0; its midpoint is −8 stops, only 0.39% of sensor saturation. JPEG charts use rendered tone values from 0 to 255, so horizontal positions in the two charts are not directly comparable. White balance and tone processing can make the JPEG reach white before the sensor saturates. Saturated values appear in a separate area at the right edge.

Highlight headroom measures the brightest channel’s 99.9th percentile against the camera/file white level, reducing the effect of a few hot pixels. Once 0.1% of any channel clips, the line changes to Clipped: 2.4%, for example. This percentage describes the channel with the highest clipped proportion, not a demosaiced pixel count. Clipping below the threshold still appears at the chart’s right edge. An entirely black signal shows “Headroom unavailable.” The measurement covers the active sensor area, independent of display crops.

JPEG selects a linear histogram of the paired JPEG, or the largest readable embedded JPEG when no readable JPEG companion exists. The source label’s tooltip identifies which file or preview is used. JPEG histograms do not report RAW highlight headroom.

Render RAW Preview, below the main photo or in the command bar, renders only the selected photo. The existing image stays visible while it loads. The photographic rendering includes camera white balance; it remains separate from the unrendered sensor histogram. Choose Show JPEG Preview to return.

Enable Show clipping on image to mark clipped regions for the selected channels and histogram source. RAW uses sensor saturation; JPEG uses full-resolution rendered values at 255. Each marker can cover several photosites so small clipped areas remain visible when zoomed out. Markers follow zoom, pan, orientation, and supported display crops. Camera JPEG crops or lens corrections can shift alignment with sensor regions. The overlay label identifies its source, including JPEG fallback. This display does not change exposure or the image file.

Compare above the histogram opens the same photo’s labeled RAW and JPEG previews side by side, with linked zoom and pan and full-resolution loading for 100% inspection. The RAW histogram stays visible. Close Comparison returns to the single preview. Navigating to another photo returns to ordinary preview loading. This differs from C, which compares multiple photographs.

If RAW data cannot be read, Ingest labels the fallback JPEG histogram and explains: “Couldn't read raw data for this file. Showing the JPEG histogram instead.” If no JPEG can be read, the module shows an unavailable state. Other rendered formats retain their own source labels. Unsupported sensor layouts and RAW compression variants use this fallback.

Calculation runs in the background and is cached by file revision. Loading holds the chart’s space; results fade in when ready. RAW rendering and analysis pause during ingest. These controls inspect exposure and rendering; they do not adjust exposure or assign ratings.

Overlays

I cycles the information panel: off, the exposure, then everything including the pixel size being displayed. That last part matters, because it tells you whether you are looking at a small preview or the full size image.

KeyOverlay
ICamera, lens, exposure, and what size image is on screen.
⌥DDetail. Turns the image into pure edge contrast, which makes soft frames obvious.
⌥EEdges, drawn in color over the photograph.
⌥AThe focus point the camera recorded, when it recorded one. Sony and Fujifilm both do; Ingest explains when the information is unavailable.

The face strip finds faces on your Mac, using Apple's Vision framework, only so it can enlarge them. No names, no identities, nothing stored, nothing sent. It runs only while the strip is open.

Bracket stacks

A three or five shot bracket is one picture taken several times. Ingest recognizes those sets and shows them as a single cell with a badge, so a card of a hundred brackets looks like twenty pictures instead of a hundred frames.

S expands or collapses the set you are on, ⌥S does all of them. A mark on a collapsed set applies to every frame in it, so tagging the bracket keeps the whole bracket.

Sets are found two ways. Sony writes a flag saying the frames were an auto bracket, which is definitive. Fujifilm does not, so Ingest looks for frames taken a fraction of a second apart with the same aperture and ISO and a clean spread of shutter speeds. Sets of three, five, seven, and nine are recognized, and an incomplete set is still stacked and marked as incomplete rather than quietly hidden.

If it gets one wrong, fix it. Select frames and combine them into a stack, or uncombine one. Your corrections are remembered for that folder. Stacking can be turned off per folder with ⌥⌘K.

A RAW and JPEG / HEIF pair is handled separately: by default the two files show as one photograph, and marking or copying acts on both. ⌥⌘J turns that off for a folder when you want to see them apart.

Video

Clips sit in the grid alongside the photographs with a poster frame and their length. Space plays. J, K, and L shuttle the way they do in every editor, , and . step a frame, and M mutes.

↑ and ↓ always move to the next item rather than doing something to the video, so a clip never traps you in the middle of a cull.

Sony clips carry a small sidecar file with the real creation time. Ingest reads it, so clips sort into the right place among the photographs, and it copies the sidecar alongside the clip when you ingest.

Culling

T toggles a tag and U removes it. Tag photos to find or act on them together. Stars, colors, and keywords are independent: 1 to 5 set stars, and pressing the current rating clears it. 6 to 9 set color labels, with ⌥9 for purple. X is unassigned.

By default a mark acts where you are and stays there, which is what Photo Mechanic does and what most people want when they are rating. Turn on advancing in Settings, Culling and the loop becomes one key per photograph: T to keep, → to skip. Holding ⇧ with any marking key does the opposite for that one press.

Everything is undoable. ⌘Z puts back a mark, a batch of marks, or a stack you took apart.

Tags live in Ingest. Stars and color labels are written into the photographs themselves, in the background, a couple of seconds after you stop typing, so Lightroom sees them when it imports. On a card nothing is written at all; the marks are remembered and applied to the copies during the ingest.

Press Delete or right-click a photo to delete it. Confirmation is enabled by default; change it in Settings or in the confirmation dialog. All shows every photo in the current folder scope, and Untagged shows every photo without a tag.

Splits

The compact photo toolbar puts All, Tagged, and Untagged first in the filter menu, with ratings, colors, Unmarked, and specialist filters below. Unmarked means no tag, stars, or color. Your saved views remain in a collapsible sidebar section. Clicking the active filter beside the folder name clears it.

The counts update as you mark, which turns culling into something you can see the end of. ⌥Tab steps through the splits.

Set up a filter you like and press ⇧⌘S to save it as your own split. Settings, Splits lets you edit one, give it an icon, or hide the built-in ones you never use.

Filters behave: they survive going in and out of the preview, they wrap where you expect, and marking the last photograph out of a view returns you to everything rather than leaving you in an empty room.

Fujifilm recipes

When browsing files and folders, select a Fujifilm JPEG to see Fujifilm Recipe below Location in Photo Info. Its collapsed heading shows the detected film simulation. Expand it to read the camera’s recorded settings or saved notes. Copy, Export, Edit, and the keyword action sit above the details. Choose Edit to change the text; Save, Cancel, and Use Camera Settings stay above the editor. JPEG companions in RAW + JPEG pairs work too.

If the photo already has saved notes, Ingest displays them as written. Otherwise, it prepares an unsaved draft from the recognized simulation and available white balance, tone, color, grain, and other settings. It leaves missing settings out and does not guess unknown simulations or custom recipe names.

Edit or replace the entire text, then choose Save to keep it with the JPEG. Cancel restores saved notes or the detected camera settings. Use Camera Settings restores the detected recipe while editing. Viewing a recipe does not write metadata. Recipe notes share the Instructions field with the full metadata editor. On a card, Save opens a short dialog to queue the notes for after ingest.

Copy copies the text currently shown. Export saves it as a .txt or .md file, including unsaved edits. These are files for sharing and reference, not profiles that can be loaded onto a camera.

Add “Provia” to Keywords names the simulation it will add. The button changes to a checkmark and confirmation when the keyword is present. Existing keywords stay in place and duplicates are avoided. With multiple photos selected, each qualifying JPEG gets its own simulation keyword; select one qualifying JPEG to edit recipe text. Recipe saves and simulation keywords affect only JPEGs, including in RAW + JPEG pairs.

On a card, recipe saves and simulation keywords are queued only after you confirm the dialog. Choose Don’t show again to skip future card recipe confirmations. Turn them back on with Confirm card recipe changes in Settings → Sidebar Information → Fujifilm Recipe. Queued changes are kept on your Mac, survive restarting Ingest, and apply to verified JPEG copies at both destinations after the ingest preset. Undo removes a queued change. Card originals and RAW companions stay untouched; Copy and Export work immediately. A queued change remains associated with its source photo for subsequent ingests until you undo it.

Use Settings → Sidebar Information to hide or reorder the section.

Metadata

Photo Info keeps Caption, Keywords, and Location close at hand. Its menu opens the full IPTC editor when you need specialized fields. Choose and reorder sidebar fields in Settings → Sidebar Information. Ingest writes supported rendered files directly and uses XMP sidecars for proprietary RAW files. Export a JPEG with metadata and GPS included for apps such as Apple Photos that may not read RAW sidecars.

Use Suggest… beside Caption or Keywords for on-device Apple Intelligence suggestions on supported macOS 27 systems. Review each photo, edit the suggested text, and choose what to save. Nothing is written until you approve.

Location accepts address/place search, decimal latitude and longitude, an Apple Maps link containing coordinates, or just city and state. Camera location describes where you stood and can set the GPS used by map apps. Place in the photo describes the subject location; photographing a skyline from across a river is one example where they differ. Saving only place names leaves GPS unchanged.

Open the inspector with ⌘I. K opens Keywords. ⇧C goes to the caption. ⇧K takes back the keywords you just added, which is the one you will want after applying the wrong thing to forty photographs.

Every write is checked. Ingest keeps the tool's backup of the file until it has read the file back and confirmed the new value, then removes the backup. If the read back does not match, the original is put back. That is why writing metadata to a hundred files takes a moment.

Never to a card. Camera originals are never modified. Set metadata in the ingest flow to apply it to verified destination copies.

Quick entry

Fields are slow. Q opens one line that takes all of it at once: coast sunrise 3* red "First light at the pier" @Ecola State Park /personal sets two keywords, three stars, a red label, a caption, a location, and a preset.

It is deliberately forgiving about order and punctuation, and the legend stays on screen while you type. The full syntax is on the naming page, along with snippets, which let you type ;addr instead of a sentence you use every day.

Presets

A metadata preset is a named set of fields to apply together: keywords, a caption, a title, your creator and copyright, and optionally a location. M shows the list with what each one would change, and ⇧⌘M applies the last one again without asking.

Each field chooses whether it replaces what is there or leaves an existing value alone, so a preset can fill in the blanks on a hundred photographs without overwriting the captions you wrote by hand. Keywords can add to what is there rather than replace it.

Captions and titles can use the tokens, so a preset with the caption {address} writes the right address on every photograph of every job, for ever, without being edited.

A preset can also be chosen in the Ingest sheet, which applies it to the copies as they arrive. That is usually the right moment, because the card stays untouched and the work happens once.

Locations

⌘L searches for an address and sets it as the job location. Choose the result and the coordinates are attached, then Apply to the selection, to everything tagged, or to everything.

Ingest writes the GPS coordinates in the EXIF fields Apple Photos reads, so a pin appears on the map, and the city, state, and country in the fields Lightroom reads, so a search for a city finds the photographs. Both at once, from one address.

⇧⌘L strips every location field back out, which is the one to know before sending photographs of your house to a stranger. Address search sends queries to Apple while you type. Software update checks also use the network. The privacy page has the detail.

Destinations

Favorites are the folders you copy into. ⌘D adds the one you are looking at, and you can drop folders onto the list from Finder. Their order matters: the first nine get a number, and that number picks them inside the Ingest sheet with ⌘1 to ⌘9 and opens them from anywhere with ⌃⌘1 to ⌃⌘9.

An ingest can write to two destinations at once. The second copy is made from the first one after it has been verified, and it is checked the same way.

Under Settings, Destinations there is a card called Ingest starts with. That is the memory of what you used last, in editable form, so a repeat card can be ⌘G and then Return.

Renaming

Names are built from templates: text plus tokens in braces. {yyyy}{mm}{dd}-{seqn} gives 20260912-7078. The full list of tokens, and how the sequence number that never repeats works, is on the naming page.

Renaming happens during an ingest, or in place afterwards with ⌘R. ⌥⌘R re-sequences what is on screen, which is how you close the gaps in the numbering after throwing half a shoot away.

Every rename records the original filename inside the file, so Undo Rename can put the old names back later, even in a folder you opened fresh.

Ingesting

⌘G opens the sheet. It fills itself in from what you did last and from the workspace you are in, so most of the time the answer is Return.

The tabs at the top choose what to copy, with the count on each one: tagged, selected, what is on screen, or everything. ⌃1 to ⌃4 pick them without the mouse. The cursor starts in the destination field, where you can type part of a favorite's name.

Below that: an optional second destination, a subfolder named by template, renaming with a live preview of the actual filenames, which files from a RAW and JPEG / HEIF pair to take, a metadata preset to apply to the copies, and what to do with the card afterwards.

What happens when you press Ingest:

  • Each file is copied to a temporary name and flushed to disk.
  • The copy is read back and compared to the source. If they differ, that file is not published and the ingest says so.
  • The verified copy is given its real name, in a way that cannot overwrite an existing file even if one appeared a moment ago.
  • A second destination is copied from the first and checked the same way.
  • Metadata and marks are written to the copies.
  • A record of the job is saved, so Ingest can tell you later which photographs from this card you have already taken.
  • Only then, if you asked for it, are files deleted from the card, and only the ones whose copies verified everywhere.

The progress strip shows copied files and verified, published files separately. Each destination gets a completion check only when it holds every planned file. If an ingest stops early, the receipt identifies what remains only on the card and offers a deliberate retry.

Canceling keeps what was already copied and verified, and the sequence number only advances by the files actually named. A card that was half emptied last time is not a problem either: the photographs you already took are marked, and the New Since Last Import split shows you the rest.

Actions

An action is a list of steps that runs when the copy is finished: open the files in Lightroom, import them into an Apple Photos album, copy them somewhere else, run a Shortcut, run a script. You choose one in the Ingest sheet under Then run.

They are built in Settings, Actions, with a button that runs one on the current selection so you can test it without waiting for a card. The detail, including what a script gets handed, is on the automation page.

Workspaces

Different shoots want different defaults. A workspace is a named set of them: the view you start in, the sort, the thumbnail size, whether brackets stack and pairs combine, and the name template, subfolder, metadata preset, and destination the Ingest sheet should start with.

⌃1 to ⌃9 switch between them. Better than that, each one can list the cameras it belongs to. Open a card and Ingest looks at what most of the photographs came from, and switches for you. A card from the Sony arrives as a job, a card from the Fuji arrives as family photographs, and you did nothing.

The palette

⌘K opens a search field over everything the app can do. Type a few letters of a command, an alias, or a category. Arrow keys move, Return runs.

Every row shows its key, so the palette teaches the keyboard while you use it. Commands that cannot run right now stay visible with the reason underneath, rather than vanishing and leaving you wondering. ⇧⌘P pins a command to the top for good.

Command descriptions explain what each action does. Settings, Command Palette controls descriptions, shortcut labels, and excluded commands.

The footer names the command Return will run. Click Actions or press ⌘K again to pin or unpin the highlighted command, copy its link, or configure its shortcut. Configure Shortcut opens that exact command in Settings. Copy Link keeps the palette open and confirms the copy. Esc closes Actions first, then the palette and returns focus to where you were.

Your favorites, workspaces, splits, presets, actions, and recent folders are all in there too, so the palette is a way to go somewhere as well as a way to do something. ? shows the shortcut sheet, and holding ⌘ for a moment shows the keys that work where you are standing.

Organize

The inspector follows Grouping, Destination, Filenames, and Destination Preview. Source-folder subfolders are the default. Enable full paths when useful, review example filenames, then use Review & Organize… to confirm Copy, Move, or Rename before execution.

Switch between Browse, Organize, and Social Export with the labeled controls at the top of the window, the View menu, or the command bar. Browse keeps your selection, filter, and scroll position.

When you first open Organize for a folder, choose Copy, Move, or Rename. Start with No Grouping, group by Tagged/Untagged, Stars, Color Label, Date, Camera, or File Type, or use a saved recipe. Choose All, Selected, or Filtered photos and whether to include subfolders. Cancel returns to Browse without creating saved work.

Add or change grouping levels, make your own groups, and move photos between them. Grouping choices do not change the original files. Review the proposed filenames and destinations before applying Copy, Move, or Rename. Existing files are preserved; conflicts must be resolved before applying changes. Memory cards must be ingested first.

Once you accept setup or edit your choices, Ingest saves that work for the folder. Returning shows Resumed saved setup and its last-edited date. Start Over opens the setup chooser again; applying a new setup can be undone and keeps the history needed to recover earlier file operations.

Save a recipe to reuse grouping and naming choices with another folder. Drafts and recipes stay on your Mac; recipes can travel with a settings export.

Social Export

Select still photos in Browse and choose Import into Social Export in the command bar or photo context menu, or press ⇧⌘E. This creates a new project in the visible photo order. Videos are excluded, and a combined RAW/JPEG pair counts as one photo.

Clicking Social Export at the top of the window, or choosing Go to Social Export, returns to your last project without importing anything. Use Add Selected Photos to append the Browse selection to an open project.

Each photo starts in its own frame. Choose Single, Pair, Row, Stack, or Grid and the arrangement updates immediately. Change the number of photos, columns, direction, or equal/proportional sizes as needed. Remaining photos stay in a partially filled final frame. ⌘Z undoes the arrangement.

Use All Frames to change the whole project, This Frame for individual appearance, or click a photo to adjust its crop. Margins, borders, backgrounds, and Fill crops update while you move their sliders. One undo reverses a gesture. Drag frames to reorder them, drag photos between frames, or use the Reorder menu and Move to New Frame. Frame-specific appearance remains independent of project-wide changes.

Choose Fit or Fill, drag a photo within its cell, and adjust its zoom. Grid cells can have common or custom aspect ratios independent of the canvas. Add text layers with fonts, alignment, colors, and backgrounds; Copy Design to All Frames optionally includes the wording. Optional snapping helps align photos and text. A Stories safe-area guide is available only on 9:16 still-image canvases, is approximate, and is never exported.

Click Export… or press ⌘E inside Social Export. Choose the folder, output width, JPEG or PNG, and JPEG quality in the export sheet. Save Beside Original Photos offers a Social Exports subfolder when all sources share a writable, non-card folder. The sheet shows the actual path, final filenames, and dimensions before saving.

Width 1080 with a 4:5 frame produces 1080 × 1350; a 9:16 Story produces 1080 × 1920. Default filenames look like Photo_4x5_001.jpg. If you need a different pattern, expand Custom Naming; the naming reference explains each token. Existing files are preserved, cancellation keeps completed exports, and Review Retry includes only unfinished frames.

Projects save automatically. Rename or duplicate them, or right-click or swipe to Archive or Delete. Use Select for bulk cleanup. Projects archive after seven inactive days and move to macOS Trash after thirty days total inactivity. Keep protects a project from automatic cleanup. Opening an archived project restores it and restarts the clock. Open projects are protected, and projects from an older version receive a fresh grace period.

Cleanup affects saved project files only. Your original photos, exported images, and shared presets stay untouched. Use Locate or Refresh when a source moves or changes. Presets include appearance and output size/format, and chosen background images travel with settings exports.

Outputs use sRGB. Single-photo exports retain capture date, camera, lens, and credits, with GPS excluded unless enabled. Collages retain only shared creator/copyright. Export destinations cannot be on a memory card.

Settings

⌘, opens a window with a search field. Everything the app stores is in there with a real control: folder and app choosers instead of typed paths, pickers instead of remembered strings, live previews for templates, snippets, and splits, and drag to reorder wherever the order decides a number key.

Edits apply as you make them, the way a Mac app should, and anything destructive is undoable with ⌘Z while the window is open. Settings, Advanced exports the whole configuration as one file to carry to another Mac, described on the automation page.

There is one row that is not a setting. On the Browsing page, Memory cards are never modified has a lock beside it instead of a switch. It is listed so that the rule is visible, not so that it can be turned off.

Keywords

A tag is one tag however it is typed, and Ingest knows every tag on every photograph it has read. Folders record their keywords as they are read, and the Photos library is read in full after it connects, so a tag that exists only in Photos completes in a folder's keyword field without opening Apple Photos. Photos keywords need macOS 27.

Settings → Keywords lists every tag with how many photographs carry it in Photos and in which folders. Select one or more and rename, merge, change case, unify spellings, remove from suggestions, or delete. Every change shows a preview first: how many photographs in Photos, how many files in each open folder, and how many files sit in folders that are not open, which keep the old spelling until you open them. Photos is changed in batches and a failed batch is reported without stopping the rest; open folders go through the verified metadata path and sit in Undo, and the receipt offers Revert.
