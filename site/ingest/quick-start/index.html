Ingest / Getting started

From your SD card to Lightroom.

Learn the flow in about five minutes. Plug in a card, tag the keepers, check the close calls, then copy and verify. Save your setup when you’re ready.

You’ll need an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 15 or later, a card reader or photo folder, and a destination with enough space. Lightroom Classic is optional. Transfer time depends on your card, drive, and files.

Start with ⌘K. The Command Palette finds commands, folders, and saved workspaces. You don’t have to memorize every shortcut.

Meet the palette →

The app shows this walkthrough on your first launch. Reopen it through Help → Walkthrough: Card to Lightroom, or type “walkthrough” in ⌘K. You can leave for Settings and return to the same step while the app stays open.

1. Insert2. Tag3. Inspect4. Copy5. Set up

  1. Plug in your SD card.

    With Ingest running, insert your camera card. A newly detected card opens in the Ingest tab when Settings → Transfers → Open a card when it is inserted is enabled. It is on by default. If the card was connected before you launched Ingest, choose it from Cards & Drives.

    Your source is on the left, photos are in the center, and your copy setup is on the right. For a photo folder, use the + beside Folders. Set To to the destination you want; no copy starts until you click Ingest.

    Find it with ⌘K: type “Ingest” or “Find Folder”.

  2. Tag your keepers.

    Use the arrow keys to move through the shoot. Press T to tag a keeper; press it again to untag. Space opens a larger Preview and returns to your previous view.

    Choose Show: Tagged above the grid to see your tagged photos. Select the photos you want, then choose Ingest From Selection…. Review the destination and file count before starting the copy. A checkmark means tagged; it is not a separate import checkbox.

    These are the default keys. Your customized shortcuts appear in the app walkthrough. See the keyboard reference or open Settings → Shortcuts.

    Find it with ⌘K: type “Tag” or “Show Tagged Only”.

  3. Check the close calls.

    Select two photos and press C for Compare. Look at the expression, focus, and fine detail, then tag your favorite.

    Default inspection shortcuts
    KeyWhat it does
    CCompare selected photos side by side.
    ZGrid loupe under the pointer; in Preview, toggle Fit / 100%.
    ⌥EToggle Focus Peaking in Preview. Green highlights strong edges; unhighlighted areas may still be sharp. Inspect the original at 100% to judge focus.
    ⌥DToggle the Detail Overlay in Preview to inspect fine detail.
    ICycle the photo information display.

    Shoot bursts or exposure brackets? Stack review keeps related frames together. You can learn that after your first import.

    Find it with ⌘K: type “Compare”, “Loupe”, “Edge”, or “Detail”.

  4. Copy, back up, and hand off to Lightroom.

    In the right sidebar, choose your To destination. If a workspace summary is showing, open Change for This Import first. For a second copy, choose Add Backup and select a folder on another drive. Ingest writes and verifies both copies in the same job.

    Before copying, open Metadata → Afterwards and select Open in Lightroom Classic. Check the file count and planned destinations, then click Ingest. After the copy succeeds, Classic opens those copied files for you to review and confirm its import.

    Classic must be installed. If its action is missing or the app lives elsewhere, configure it in Settings → Automation. You can also import the destination folder from Classic yourself. Set up the Lightroom handoff →

    For your first import, keep Leave the card alone. Wait for the verification result before ejecting. Understand copy and verification results →

  5. Ready for an advanced setup?

    Create a workspace for each kind of photography in Settings → Workspaces. Save destinations, backups, naming, metadata, and viewing preferences instead of rebuilding the same job every time.

    • Personal: simple folders and optional Apple Photos.
    • Real Estate: address-based names and exposure brackets.
    • Weddings: dated jobs, keywords, and a backup.

    Set up workspaces →   Metadata presets →   Automation →

    Apple Photos has a global switch in Settings → General and a switch for each workspace. Keep it on for Personal and off for Real Estate; the global switch must also be on. Learn about Apple Photos →

    One shortcut to keep: ⌘K. Type “Workspace” to find your saved setups, “walkthrough” to revisit these steps, or the name of whatever you want to do next.

Try it at your own pace.
