0.8.0

Apple Photos is a source, not a second window

  • Choose Library, Favorites, Unfiled, Unreviewed, or any album in the sources list and the photographs swap in place. The sidebar width, the inspector, and the tab you were on stay where they were.
  • One set of commands covers files and Photos. A command that cannot apply says why rather than disappearing, and a shortcut you remapped applies in both places.
  • Turn Apple Photos integration off in Settings → General and the source is simply absent.

A photo bar you can read

  • Sort shows what the order is and turns it around with the button beside it. Sort by capture time, filename, rating, color label, date modified, or file size; Apple Photos adds album order and Organize adds group order.
  • Filter is a panel rather than a menu: what is shown, a minimum rating, any combination of color labels, and a kind such as brackets or video.
  • Everything you have chosen appears as chips under the bar, each with a cross, beside a count of how many photographs are showing and a Clear.
  • The search field is always visible, and a folder now reopens in the order you left it in.

Keywords are one pool

  • Settings → Keywords lists every tag with how many photographs carry it in Photos and in which folders, and sorts by any column.
  • Rename, merge, change case, unify spellings, or delete a tag everywhere at once, after a preview that says exactly what will change.
  • Fixes a merge into one of a tag's own spellings that removed the tag from the photographs in Apple Photos instead of keeping it.

New typography

  • Headings, photo names, and folder names are set in IBM Plex Sans. Filenames, exposure readouts, counts, and keyboard hints are set in iA Writer Mono, which is drawn from the same family.
  • Controls, captions, and hints stay in the macOS system font, so they match the menus and alerts the system draws.

macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon. Native Photos captions, keywords, and star ratings require macOS 27. Read the Photos guide.

0.7.0

Apple Photos, integrated and optional

  • Browse the System Photo Library, favorites, ordinary albums, and Unfiled and Unreviewed inboxes. Search albums, preview photos, and review selections with the keyboard.
  • Create and organize albums and folders, file photos into several albums, adjust order, and preview additive grouping recipes before applying them.
  • Edit native favorites, dates, and locations. Native captions, keywords, and ratings are available on macOS 27; earlier systems retain local ratings. Tags, colors, and review marks stay local.
  • Send JPEGs or JPEG companions from file workflows through a reviewed import sheet, with explicit RAW options and content receipts that reuse confirmed imports. Interrupted submissions require reconciliation or an intentional retry.
  • Bring Photos selections into Social Export using retained copies of the current rendered images, and send completed Social JPEGs back to Photos.
  • Keep Apple Photos integration on by default, or turn it off in Settings → General to hide its source, commands, and actions. Permission is requested when connecting or sending.
  • Keep album removal separate from confirmed library deletion. Photos changes follow your iCloud settings.

This remains a working beta. Shared Album publishing, Smart Album rule editing, and continuous rule synchronization are not included. Read the Photos guide and updated privacy details.

0.5.0

A faster way to browse folders, a simpler tagging workflow, and more control over Social Export.

Browse and review

  • Browse your Mac's folder tree inside Ingest, search folders, and open a folder with a single selection. Leaf folders no longer show misleading disclosure arrows.
  • Show photos in subfolders when you need them. The visible toggle defaults off, and selecting a photo identifies its containing folder in the tree.
  • Open a photo from Finder with Ingest to jump to its folder and select it.
  • Zoom the grid all the way to one photo. Improved pinch sizing keeps cached previews visible; arrow navigation stays with photos by default.
  • Restore compact Grid, Preview, and Split icons. Press I or use the info button to cycle basic, full, or hidden photo information in all three views.
  • Inspect RGB or luminance histograms. Histogram, Camera Details, Keywords, Caption, and Location open expanded and remember later disclosure choices.
  • Metadata shortcuts reveal, scroll to, and focus their fields. Location updates preserve the current view, selection, zoom, and cached photos instead of reopening the folder.
  • Clear an active filter from the folder header. Command descriptions explain overlays and navigation; AF Point Overlay reports when focus-point information is unavailable.

Tagging and deletion

  • Use Tag/Untag as a simple flag: T toggles and U untags. Stars, colors, and keywords stay independent. Reject and its shortcuts, filters, badges, and cleanup action have been removed; X is unassigned.
  • All includes every photo in the current scope; Untagged includes every photo without a tag. Unmarked describes photos without tags, stars, or colors.
  • Delete selected photos with the Delete key or a context menu. Confirmation is on by default, with a preference available both in Settings and in the confirmation dialog.

Organize

  • Group by Tagged/Untagged, star rating, color label, and the existing date, camera, lens, file type, and keyword choices. Grouping is available in the initial setup and inspector.
  • A clearer inspector follows Grouping, Destination, Filenames, and Destination Preview. Source-folder subfolders are the default; alternate destinations show their folder chooser explicitly.
  • Preview full paths when useful and see three example filenames while renaming. Advanced date options stay out of the main flow.
  • Review & Organize is a prominent fixed action, with Copy, Move, or Rename identified before execution. Background planning keeps previews stable while typing.

Social Export

  • Choose Fit or Fill for a photo, drag its position, and zoom within its cell. Grid cells support common and custom aspect ratios independently of the canvas, with remaining space kept as background.
  • Add editable text layers to individual frames with fonts, alignment, color, and optional backgrounds. Copy a text design to all frames, optionally including its wording.
  • Snap photos and text to centers and edges. An optional approximate Stories safe-area guide appears only on 9:16 still-image canvases and is never included in exported files.

A quieter native interface

  • A wider resizable sidebar keeps folder names readable. Favorites, Recent, and Saved Views collapse independently and remember their state.
  • Compact photo controls combine view, sizing, sort, and filters; secondary controls move into an overflow menu at narrow widths.
  • Archivo headings retain Ingest's character, with more readable system-font controls and restrained orange accents. Browse, Organize, and Social Export have distinct active colors, and a labeled Ingest… button opens the import flow.
  • Removed the oversized orange pane-focus frame while retaining focus feedback on individual controls.

Existing IPTC capabilities are unchanged in this release. Requires macOS 15 or later and Apple silicon.

0.4.1

Immediate previews and clearer ways to organize and export.

  • Social Export updates previews while you move sliders or adjust crops. Each gesture is one undo step, and cached previews appear before higher-resolution refinement.
  • Changing Single, Pair, Row, Stack, or Grid automatically rearranges photos. Crops and photo order stay intact; there is no separate Arrange Photos button.
  • Labeled Browse, Organize, and Social Export controls stay visible. Go to Social Export resumes a project; Import into Social Export and ⇧⌘E create a new project from selected photos.
  • The Preview filmstrip visibly supports Command-click and Shift selection, including Shift-arrow ranges.
  • A prominent Export button and ⌘E open an export sheet with folder, size, quality, and final filename previews. Custom naming tokens stay in an optional disclosure.
  • Archive, Keep, Delete, and bulk cleanup make projects easier to manage. Projects archive after seven inactive days and move to macOS Trash after thirty; Keep protects them. Older projects get a fresh grace period.
  • Organize starts with a setup chooser for Copy, Move, Rename, and grouping. No Grouping is the default. Deliberate saved work resumes automatically with a Start Over option; merely opening a folder does not count as saved work.

Original photos remain unchanged by Social Export and project cleanup. The updated guide includes new native Retina screenshots of both workspaces.

0.4.0

Social Export and Organize workspaces.

  • Open Social Export with ⇧⌘E to compose selected still photos as Single, Pair, Row, Stack, or Grid frames. Adjust Fit/Fill crops, ratios, margins, spacing, borders, and automatic or chosen backgrounds.
  • Save source-linked projects and reusable presets. Reopening a project preserves its photo order, crops, and appearance. Presets and their background images travel with settings export/import.
  • Review filenames and dimensions before exporting sRGB JPEG or opaque PNG images. Width 1080 with a 4:5 ratio produces 1080 × 1350 files. GPS is excluded by default; collages keep shared credits only.
  • Existing export files are preserved. Cancel keeps completed files, and retry reviews only unfinished frames using refreshed source links.
  • Organize groups existing folders by date, camera, lens, file type, or keywords. Review Copy, Move, and Rename operations, save recipes, and restore drafts. Memory cards must be ingested before organization.
  • Simplifies job dialogs and Settings navigation, improves Light and Dark appearance, and keeps workspace keyboard focus and undo histories separate.

Social Export leaves original photos unchanged. This release does not include carousel splitting, video export, freeform layouts, watermarks, or transparent output.

0.3.0

More cameras, with reliable previews and safer imports.

  • Adds camera support for Canon, Nikon, Hasselblad, Panasonic / Lumix, OM System / Olympus, Ricoh / Pentax, and Leica alongside the existing Sony and Fujifilm readers. JPEG and HEIC / HEIF / HIF companions travel with their RAW files.
  • Prefers camera previews and paired images. Files that need RAW rendering use a bundled fallback, isolated from normal browsing. No separate decoder installation is needed.
  • Proprietary RAW metadata goes into XMP companions. External edits refresh in an open folder while pending local ratings are preserved; rating-only edits keep cached previews.
  • Improves cancellation, eject coordination, and recovery after an interrupted import. Verified copies are retained, receipt-save errors keep originals, and retries do not automatically delete source files.
  • Preserves the Sony / Fuji preview paths and streaming ingest. Mixed-format samples, large-folder metadata, disposable interruption checks, and repeated preview benchmarks are covered by validation.

Camera support depends on model and recording mode. The tested Nikon Z8 compression mode cannot be rendered by bundled LibRaw; its embedded preview works, and native rendering works on the tested macOS 27 system. Runtime on macOS 15, physically cold-card behavior, and instrumented display-frame timing remain unverified. See supported formats and limitations.

0.2.1

The same Studio design, with more reliable Mac controls.

  • Select All works in the command palette, Settings, and sheets. Undo and redo stay in the field you are editing, even when its history is empty.
  • The palette keeps its search field still as results change. The footer shows the highlighted command and an Actions button for Pin/Unpin, Copy Link, and Configure Shortcut.
  • Inside the palette, ⌘K opens Actions. Escape closes Actions first, then the palette. Configure Shortcut takes you directly to the command in Settings.
  • Shortcut recorders support keyboard focus, Return or Space to start, and Escape to cancel, with named accessibility controls. Standard Mac editing keys stay protected.
  • Filename previews stay readable in Light appearance. Hints, hover states, focus rings, and floating-panel shadows adapt to appearance and accessibility display settings.
  • Accent controls update immediately when you choose a color. Archivo, the four accents, the black photo stage, and the existing layout are preserved.

0.2.0

A new look, and a receipt that tells you when the card can leave.

  • Ingest is set in Archivo, with one type scale across every screen. Filenames, paths, counts, and byte readouts stay in monospace, so the machine keeps speaking in one voice and people in another.
  • The inspector is sections rather than a stack of labels. The filename sits above a four-line block of what the camera recorded, and marks, stars, keywords, caption, and location each get their own space.
  • ⌘K groups results under headings. A command that cannot run keeps its place and prints the reason beneath it, so the list never shortens under the arrow keys.
  • Ingesting shows both of its stages at once. One band is what has come off the card; the solid band behind it is what has been read back, verified, and written under its final name. The card is safe to remove when the bar is one color.
  • Each destination reports its own count, and a green tick appears only once that destination holds every file.
  • A run ends with a receipt: how many items, how fast, the first and last filename written, and what happened to the card.
  • When a run does not finish cleanly, the receipt says how many files exist only on the card and offers to ingest those. Retrying is always a choice, never automatic — a bad read is the clearest evidence a card is failing, and that is worth knowing rather than quietly working around.
  • Eject when done no longer fires if files were left behind. Ejecting is never blocked; the button is right there on the receipt.
  • Every commit sheet names its action and its count: Ingest 86 items rather than Ingest.

Memory cards are still never written to. Verification, the journal, and undo are unchanged — this release changes how the app looks and what it tells you, not what it does to your files.

0.1.0

The first release.

  • Browse a card or folder at full speed. Sony ARW and Fujifilm RAF are read through the JPEG the camera embedded, so photographs appear immediately and arrow keys never wait. JPEG, HEIC, and video work too.
  • Cull from the keyboard: tag, reject, five star ratings, and the Lightroom color labels, all undoable. Compare two frames or survey four with the zoom locked across the panes.
  • Exposure brackets of three, five, seven, and nine frames are found and shown as one cell. RAW and JPEG pairs become one photograph, and both settings can be changed per folder.
  • Saved views in the sidebar with live counts, so a cull has a visible end. Workspaces carry a set of defaults for a kind of shoot and can switch themselves by camera model.
  • Ingest copies to one folder or two, reads each copy back and compares it to the source before publishing it, and never overwrites anything. Files leave a card only after their copies verified.
  • Name templates with the brace tokens and a sequence number that never repeats, with a preview of the real filenames before anything is written.
  • Keywords, captions, titles, ratings, color labels, and GPS written into the files in the fields Lightroom and Apple Photos read, verified by reading them back. Quick entry sets all of it from one line.
  • Actions that run after a copy, an ingest:// URL scheme, and a settings file that carries your whole setup to another Mac.
  • Every command is in ⌘K, and every key can be changed in Settings.

Memory cards are never written to. That rule holds however a card was opened, and it is not a preference.
