Most family recipe cards are the only copy.

Pencil fades. Cards get splattered, stuck together, and lost in a move. When the box gets split between cousins, half the recipes go with it. Once a card is gone, nobody can ask for the recipe again.

Copying them into a notes app loses the card itself, and typing out a few dozen recipes by hand rarely gets finished.

Save a recipe card in three steps.

  1. Photograph the card

    Use the camera or pick a photo you already took. Both sides, if the recipe runs over.

  2. Check what it read

    The text sits next to the photo. Fix a smudged number or a name it got wrong.

  3. Cook from it

    Cross off ingredients as you add them and check off each step. The card is one tap away.

Keep every recipe you cook in one recipe box.

Find it by who gave it to you

Each recipe says where it came from. Search “Grandma” and her recipes come up.

Add recipes from websites

Paste a link. You get the ingredients and directions, with a link back to the page.

Add recipes from Instagram

Paste the caption, or import a screenshot when the recipe is in the picture.

Send a copy to family

Share a recipe as a Faded file with the card photos, a PDF, or plain text.

Your recipes stay on your device.

There’s no account to make and no server holding your recipe box. Handwriting is read with Apple’s text recognition and Apple Intelligence. Read the privacy policy

It doesn’t plan meals or suggest recipes.

No meal planner, shopping list, or feed of recipes to try. Faded Recipes holds the recipes your family actually makes, and that’s all it does.
