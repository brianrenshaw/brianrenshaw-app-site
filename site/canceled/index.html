The quiet way out · iPhone and iPad

Nobody has to be the one who cancels.

Some nights you both want to stay home and neither of you will say it first. So you go, and you both call it lovely. Canceled lets you each say it privately instead. If you both do, the plan is off. If only one of you does, nobody ever finds out you wavered.

In development for iPhone and iPad. Nothing to install yet. The irony has been noted.

01 / Private

Nobody sees you flinch.

Choose Cancel and nothing changes on your friend’s screen. The app’s own summary: they see nothing, and the plan is still, infuriatingly, on. Their choice never reaches your device either. You can change your mind until you both cancel or the evening starts.

02 / Mutual

Two cancels and the night is yours.

When you both choose Cancel, the plan comes off and you find out together. The app files it under “Canceled. Beautifully.” Neither of you had to be the one who asked.

03 / One app

Only one of you needs it.

Send a link. Your friend accepts and makes their own private choice in a browser, with nothing to install. Plans can come from your calendar too, which, the app notes, still thinks you’re going. Bold of it. Calendar access is opt-in and starts with nothing selected.

The plan is still on unless you both cancel.

One choice stays private. Two cancels produce a shared cancellation.

The reveal

Your evening has been returned to you.

That is what it actually says when you both cancel, above a red stamp and a short, smug audit of the night: one evening returned, one hour thirty. Excuses used, none.

Nobody sent the awkward text. Nobody invented a sick relative. As the app puts it, you both wanted out, which is excellent communication, technically.

120 ways to lose your nerve

It never says the same thing twice.

Every private cancel comes with its own line, drawn from a bank of 120 and shuffled so you don’t get a repeat. There is a whole bureaucracy in there, and a furniture lobby.

  • The sweatpants lobby has made its opening argument.
  • Your request is with the Ministry of Maybe Not.
  • Your calendar booked the deluxe version of you.
  • The plan sounded different before the shoes became involved.
  • Imagine two people separately discovering the merits of soup.
  • The trailer promised adventure. The audience has notes.

Elsewhere: an empty schedule reads “No plans. Suspiciously efficient.” Keeping one gets you “Look at you, honoring a commitment.” The past tab promises no scores and no judgment. Well, very little.

Still being built

Two friends. One mutual understanding.

Canceled is a personal project, still in development for iPhone and iPad. There is nothing to install yet and no date to promise, which your couch will no doubt spin as good news.
