Walkthrough / The guide

Group, name, and process listing photos.

Open a folder of listing photos, assign rooms, arrange their order, and review the filenames before processing. This guide explains the workspace, command bar, naming presets, room templates, and processing options.

Start with a photo folder

Choose Open or press ⌘O and select a folder containing JPG or JPEG files. New imports use photos directly inside that folder. Walkthrough saves the job and its organization in this Mac’s private library. The five-step Getting Started guide uses real app screenshots to cover Open, Group, Order, Name, and Process Photos. Enlarge screenshots or switch between detail and full views. Replay it anytime from Help → Getting Started.

The room sidebar has All Photos, Ungrouped, and your ordered rooms, with a photo count beside each. Select a room to work with just its photos. Arrow keys, range selection, Select All, and the preview filmstrip follow that scope. Switching rooms clears the previous selection. All Photos keeps the complete listing available, including collapsed groups.

Choose Contact Sheet, Preview, or Split in the toolbar. Drag the native dividers to resize panes. Rooms and Inspector buttons show or hide the side panes; a pane may collapse at smaller widths to preserve space for photos. The inspector contains selection actions, original and output filenames, naming presets, templates, and listing details. Its pinned Process X Photos button counts all included photos in the job, independent of room or selection.

Enter the listing address, select photos, and press G to choose a group or create one. Command-click adds or removes individual photos; Shift-click selects a range. Drag photos onto a sidebar room to assign them, or onto Ungrouped to remove their grouping.

Drag rooms in the sidebar, use their context menu, or press ⇧G to arrange the delivery order. In the order editor, Up/Down chooses a room, Option-Up/Down moves it, and P lets you enter a position. Apply Order updates the filename sequence. Grouping, group renaming, ordering, and preset/template application support Command-Z.

Folder changes, exclusions, and Trash

Open jobs watch their photo folders. New top-level JPG/JPEG files appear in Ungrouped; deleted photos disappear; renamed or edited photos retain their grouping and refresh their previews. Use File → Refresh Photos (Command-R) to check immediately. Existing tracked photos can move into subfolders without importing unrelated nested files. If the folder is unavailable, the saved inventory stays intact until it reconnects.

Select one or more photos, then use the Photos menu, Command-K, or the photo’s right-click menu:

  • Exclude from Job leaves the file on disk and removes it from grouping, numbering, and process. Choose Excluded in the sidebar and Include in Job to append it to Ungrouped. Both actions support Undo. Including a retained photo outside the current folder copies it into that folder without overwriting an existing file.
  • Reveal in Finder selects available source files in Finder.
  • Move to Trash (Command-Delete) uses macOS Trash and removes only successfully trashed photos from the job. Restore with Finder’s Put Back, then refresh the job.

Find an action with Command-K

Press ⌘K, choose Commands in the toolbar, or use the menu command. The palette opens in the center of the window, with search ready for typing: search is case-insensitive and accepts partial and approximate matches. Results include actions, room groups, tags, naming presets, templates, and recent jobs.

Use Up/Down to choose a result and Return to run it, or click a row. The footer names the action Return will perform. From Home, suggestions include opening a folder, Settings, help, and recent jobs. Escape or clicking outside dismisses the palette without acting and returns you to your previous focus. Unavailable actions explain what is missing, such as a selection.

Go to Kitchen selects Kitchen in the sidebar and shows only that room in Contact Sheet. Add selected photos to Kitchen changes the selected photos’ group. These are separate actions. Process opens the review dialog; it does not immediately write files. Recent jobs reopen through the same restore behavior as the Open menu.

Search for actions and saved destinations, rather than individual photo filenames. The palette does not interpret requests with AI.

Change a group name from the palette

  1. Select a photo in the group you want to rename, or select the room in the sidebar.
  2. Press ⌘K and type change group name or rename group.
  3. Choose Change Group Name. The current name is filled in.
  4. Type the new name and press Return or choose Rename Group.

For example, select a Garage photo, open the command, and enter Outside Garage. The room label and filename previews update together. Photos keep their order and stay in that group. The files on disk change when you process.

Escape or Cancel leaves the name unchanged; Command-Z undoes a completed rename. If your selection spans more than one group, select photos from one group first. Double-clicking a room name or choosing Rename Room from its context menu also works. This changes the current job’s group name, not the reusable tag in Settings.

Move Earlier/Later works when all selected photos belong to one group or to Ungrouped. Each selected run moves one position while keeping its internal order. Moves stop at section boundaries, never cross groups, and each successful invocation is one undo step. In All Photos, Select All also includes photos in collapsed groups. In one room, it selects only that room’s photos.

Settings and saving

Open Settings with ⌘, or search for Settings in the command palette. The sidebar has Tags, Presets, Templates, and General. Resize the window or scroll the editor to reach every option; the save status remains visible in the footer.

Valid changes save automatically after you finish typing. The footer shows the save status. Invalid shortcuts stay in the editor with an explanation beside the affected field; correct them before closing. If a save fails, your edits remain available with Retry. Closing Settings or quitting waits for pending saves, and asks how to handle invalid or unsaved edits. Appearance and update preferences apply immediately. Standard text editing works throughout Settings: Command-A selects the field’s text, Command-C/V copies and pastes, Command-Z undoes typing, and Tab advances focus.

Tags: reusable room names

Add common room names such as Kitchen, Living Room, or Front Exterior. Edit each name, reorder the list with the arrow controls, or delete entries you no longer need. Tags give you reusable destinations for grouping and ingredients for templates.

Optionally enter one A–Z letter in the shortcut field. With photos selected in the workspace, that letter assigns them to the matching tag group, creating the group when needed. Tags do not receive shortcuts automatically.

G, U, T, E, and Z are reserved. Two tags cannot use the same letter. Remove or change a conflicting letter so the edit can save; the inline error tells you why it is unavailable.

Presets: build a filename format

A naming preset defines the filename recipe. Select a preset to edit it; use the list controls to add, duplicate, delete, or reorder presets. At least one preset must remain. Make Default chooses the preset for new jobs. The first nine presets in list order are available with Command-1 through Command-9.

Each building block has its own row, with room for its value and separator. Drag rows or use the up/down controls to reorder them, remove unneeded blocks, and add new ones. Each block’s separator follows that block: underscore, hyphen, period, space, none, or custom text. Empty values omit their following separator too.

Business
Uses Filename name from General when supplied; otherwise uses Business name.
Address, Unit, City, State, ZIP
Use the listing information from the current job. Unit is a separate building block.
Template
Includes the applied template’s name.
Shoot Date
Uses the job’s shoot date and the preset’s date format.
Group Number, Tag, Photo Number
Include the group position, group name, and photo position within the group.
Original
Uses the original camera filename without its extension.
Custom Text
Adds a fixed label that you supply.

Number padding: choose 1, 01, or 001 independently for group and photo numbers. Enable numbering for single-photo groups if you want their filenames to include a photo number too.

Text formatting: choose the replacement for spaces, filename case (as typed, title, lower, or upper), and extension case (keep, lower, or upper). Date formats include yyyy-MM-dd, MM-dd-yy, and yyyyMMdd; use the preview to check a custom format.

The Standard pattern produces names such as 123-Oak-St_01_Entry-02.jpg. Leading zeros preserve numeric sequence when files are sorted by name. In the inspector, Edit Filename Format changes the job’s format; use Update Preset or Save as New when you want a reusable library preset.

Templates: repeat a delivery order

Templates combine an ordered set of tags with an optional naming preset. Add or duplicate a template, name it for a realtor or delivery workflow, and optionally make it the default for new jobs.

Choose a filename preset, or Keep current preset. Use Add Tag to Order, then arrange tags with the move controls or by dragging. An optional output-name override changes a tag’s output label for that template. Remove a tag from the order when it is no longer needed.

Groups with tags outside the template follow the listed tags in their existing order. A manually named group retains its manual name. Apply a template to the current job with T or through the command palette; applying a template is undoable.

General: appearance, business, updates, and library

Appearance
Choose System, Light, or Dark. New installations use System; existing choices are preserved. This applies immediately. System follows your Mac’s appearance. Teal is the default accent; Amber, Sky, and Clay are also available. The photo canvas stays dark in each appearance so image colors remain easy to judge. Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, and increased contrast are respected where applicable.
Business
Set your Business name and an optional Filename name. The shorter filename value is used by the Business naming block when it contains usable text; otherwise the business name is used. These edits save automatically. Applying a preset or template and reopening a job uses your current saved business profile. If the Business block has no usable name, the filename preview explains where to add one.
Photo Processing
Processing starts with Rename only. Choose Copy or Move in Process Photos; there is no default-copy preference to save.
Updates
Choose whether to check automatically, check manually, or open release notes. Update preferences apply immediately.
Library
Show Library in Finder opens the local app-data folder. Job history lives on this Mac; there are no sidecar job files or account requirements. Photos remain in the folders you choose.

Import and export your settings

Use the secondary menu in the Settings footer to Import Settings or Export Settings. A .listingnamersettings file carries your reusable configuration, not photographs or job history.

Export requires valid settings. Import validates the file and lets you choose Merge or Replace. Merge keeps unrelated library items and updates matching entries; Replace adopts the imported library. Confirming the import applies and saves that choice. Review the imported tags, presets, and templates afterward.

Process: rename, copy, or move

Press E, ⇧⌘E, or choose Process in the toolbar. The pinned Process X Photos inspector button opens the same review. Process Photos includes active photos in the entire job, regardless of the selected room or photos. Excluded and Needs Attention photos stay out until you include or retry them. Review opens with Rename only every time and applies the current filename preset in all three modes. The Current path and Destination path table has resizable columns; hover over a truncated path to see it in full.

  • Rename only: changes filenames inside each photo’s current directory. It does not relocate photos or rename existing folders.
  • Copy: writes the named photos to the selected output location and keeps source files. The open job follows the new copies.
  • Move: relocates the photos to the selected output location. The open job follows the moved files.

Choose a location and folder layout

Copy and Move offer Current folder or Another folder, followed by Choose Destination. Create folders from groups starts enabled. Group folders sit directly below the destination, for example 01 Front Exterior and 02 Living Room. Ungrouped photos come last; unnamed groups use Group. Number prefixes distinguish identically named groups, and padding expands for larger group counts.

Turn Create folders from groups off for a flat output. After an earlier grouped operation, Rename only keeps each photo in its existing directory; use Copy or Move with a flat layout to bring the output together.

Review before writing

Check the ready, skipped, and total counts and every output path. Continue with these naming warnings acknowledges missing listing details or unnamed/ungrouped photos. It does not resolve file errors.

Skip photos with errors starts unchecked. Enable it to process eligible photos while skipping missing, unreadable, or replaced sources, invalid names, and destination conflicts. Every photo involved in a duplicate destination is skipped. At least one photo must be ready, naming warnings must be acknowledged, and the destination folder must be available. The dialog explains why processing is disabled.

Walkthrough never overwrites existing files or silently adds suffixes. Skipped photos keep the names and numbering shown in the preview, so gaps are possible. JPG bytes are preserved; processing does not re-encode images.

Follow processing progress

The confirmation button includes the number of photos, such as Rename 100 Photos. After you confirm, the review sheet closes and the workspace shows two progress bands: photos staged and photos written under their final names.

Finish and retry skipped photos

The result reports processed and skipped counts with filenames and reasons. Choose Needs Attention in the sidebar, fix the source or destination issue, refresh, select the failed photos, and choose Retry Selected. Review their retained destinations before retrying; successful retries return to the active job and their original group where possible.

After a partial Copy or Move, the job follows the successfully processed files while failed photos keep their original locations. If nothing succeeds, the active folder stays unchanged. Missing Needs Attention records remain available to retry. If a retained target belongs to a previous job folder, Include in Job brings the photo into the current folder for a fresh processing run.

Review grouping, ordering, and naming with Undo before processing. A completed operation clears workspace undo history; Command-Z does not reverse a completed filesystem operation. Sort delivered files by name and check their final order in the MLS or website after upload.

Reopen jobs and find moved folders

Use Command-N or Command-T to open a new job tab without closing your current job. Command-W closes the selected tab. The Home toolbar button returns the current tab to your recent jobs.

Remove an entry from Recents with its × button, right-click menu, or the folder menu. Its saved rooms and photo order stay in Walkthrough and return when you reopen the folder. Opening jobs and returning to the app also check for folder changes.

Open a recent job from the Open menu or command palette. Jobs processed into group folders reopen using their tracked paths, including nested photographs. Restore does not import unrelated files from those subfolders.

If a folder has moved outside Walkthrough, use File → Locate Job Folder. Opening an ordinary photo folder on another Mac starts a new job; local organization history does not travel with the photographs. For help, see Support.
