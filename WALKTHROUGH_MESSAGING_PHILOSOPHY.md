# Walkthrough messaging philosophy

Recorded September 20, 2026 from Brian’s direction. Use this document when writing or revising Walkthrough’s website and product copy. See the Walkthrough section of [MESSAGING.md](MESSAGING.md) for product facts and positioning.

## Explain what the app does

> “Explanations not marketing gimmicks.”

Write as though you are explaining Walkthrough to a realtor or listing assistant who needs to organize listing photos. State what they can do, how the tools work, and what gets exported.

Each sentence should help the reader understand the app. Remove lines that add only enthusiasm, atmosphere, or a vague promise. Short copy still needs to say something useful.

## Describe the actual work

Walkthrough lets people sort photos, group them by room, arrange the groups, and export numbered filenames. Explain that workflow directly:

> Open a folder of listing photos, group them by room, and arrange the photos in the order you want. Preview the filenames before exporting numbered copies.

Explain how the interface supports that work:

> Press ⌘K to find commands for assigning photos to rooms, applying presets, and renaming groups.

Describe reuse and sharing precisely:

> Save room orders and naming presets for later jobs. Export your settings to a file that someone else can import.

Do not imply that sharing settings synchronizes apps, shares photos, or transfers job history.

## Use headings and captions that explain

Headings should name the capability or subject. Captions should describe what the screenshot shows.

| Avoid | Use instead |
| --- | --- |
| Set an order that works every time. | Save room orders as templates. |
| Stay with the photos. | Assign rooms and run commands with the keyboard. |
| Set it up once. Keep it consistent. | Reuse tags, naming presets, and room templates. |
| One folder to a finished listing. | Review filenames before exporting. |

These examples demonstrate the approach; they are not mandatory wording. Do not repeat the same list of features throughout the page.

## Avoid invented frustration and exaggerated promises

Do not tell the reader how frustrating their work must be or invent a dramatic scenario to justify the app. Explain the task without making unsupported claims about other software or the reader’s experience.

The passage Brian supplied as a rejected writing example names Ingest:

> **Every frame, the moment the card goes in.**
>
> Ingest never develops a raw file. It finds the JPEG the camera already wrote inside the ARW or RAF and shows you that, so arrowing through a full card feels like arrowing through thumbnails. No import step, no waiting for a background process to catch up.

Keep this as a style example only. It is not a description of Walkthrough’s capabilities. The immediate-results headline, strained thumbnail comparison, and absolute claims about waiting are the kind of writing to avoid.

For Walkthrough, explain observable behavior instead:

> Drag photos between rooms or change the order of the rooms. The filename previews update to match the order you set.

Do not promise that every MLS or website will preserve an upload order. Explain that numbered filenames support sorting by name, and tell users to check the final order after uploading.

## Include technical details when they help

A detail belongs in the copy when it explains what the app does, what the output contains, or a relevant limitation. For example, explain that settings are shared by exporting and importing a file. Leave exhaustive shortcut lists and naming-token syntax in the guide.

Preserve meaningful distinctions: exporting settings is not cloud sync; numbered filenames do not control every uploader; arranging listing photos does not publish the listing.

## State availability plainly

Use verified information about the current Walkthrough release. Do not borrow another app’s beta status, system requirements, file-format support, or development plans.

First-person language is appropriate when Brian is describing his own use or intentions. Say what is working and what is being developed without vague qualifications such as “young” or unsupported promises about future availability.

## Keep the voice natural

Use concrete verbs: open, select, group, drag, sort, name, preview, export, and import. Prefer ordinary sentences over clever fragments, dramatic contrasts, or generic claims about ease and speed. No em dashes, following the site’s editorial rule.

The goal is a clear explanation of useful software. Readers can decide whether those capabilities fit their work.

## Review before publishing

- Does each section explain a capability, an interaction, or a relevant limitation?
- Can the reader picture what they would do in Walkthrough?
- Is every claim supported by the current app?
- Does the heading name the subject, and does the caption explain the screenshot?
- Can any slogan, invented frustration, repetition, or unsupported promise be removed?

This philosophy governs new Walkthrough copy. Older messaging examples should not preserve a gimmicky tone simply because they were previously approved.
