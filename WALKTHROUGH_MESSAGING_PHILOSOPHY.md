# Walkthrough messaging philosophy

Updated October 16, 2026 from Brian’s request for StoryBrand messaging with all features visible. This supersedes the September 20 instruction to avoid StoryBrand framing. Photographers and real estate agents receive equal emphasis.

## Start with the customer’s outcome

The customer has listing photos and needs a set that is clear, organized, and ready to hand off or upload. Walkthrough guides that work; the app’s feature inventory is supporting evidence.

Primary promise: **Listing photos, ready to share.**

Supporting copy: Put the rooms in order, make the property clear, and deliver consistently named files. Walkthrough brings the final steps together on your Mac.

Use [StoryBrand’s customer-as-hero framework](https://storybrand.com/downloads/your-brand-is-not-the-hero.pdf) to organize the story, without exposing framework labels to website visitors.

## Acknowledge the actual preparation work

The photos still need an order, useful context, appropriate framing, and consistent filenames. Describe those real tasks. Avoid invented customer stories, dramatic anxiety, fabricated testimonials, unmeasured time savings, and claims that Walkthrough sells homes faster.

The stakes are the clarity and completeness of the handoff. Do not imply that the app publishes listings, verifies parcel boundaries, or controls how every MLS sorts uploads.

## Give people a simple plan

1. **Open:** bring the JPG/JPEG photo folder and add the listing address.
2. **Prepare:** arrange rooms and photos; add outlines, labels, nearby places, or crops where useful.
3. **Deliver:** review filenames and destinations, then rename, copy, or move the set.

The direct call to action is **Download for Mac**. The supporting action is **See how it works**. State macOS requirements and account requirements beside the download.

## Prove the story with the features

The homepage has three illustrated chapters: order the rooms, clarify the property, and finish the delivery. A complete feature index makes every capability easy to find:

- Room grouping and photo order.
- Manually drawn property outlines and styling.
- Labels, pins, arrows, off-frame callouts, and compass.
- Nearby places with Apple Maps travel estimates.
- Crop, straighten, rotate, and flip.
- Naming presets and reusable room templates.
- Rename, Copy, Move, and numbered room folders.
- Command bar and keyboard actions.
- Local saved jobs and settings-file sharing.

Use Brian’s supplied drone original, outlined result, and actual editor screenshot. Reuse genuine native workspace captures. Never fabricate app interfaces, parcel data, customers, ratings, or testimonials.

## Keep the product distinctions clear

Numbered filenames support sorting by name. Users still check the final order after uploading to an MLS or website.

Rename changes filenames in place; Copy retains source files; Move relocates them. These three actions preserve the JPG image data. Outline and crop editing creates derived JPGs and retains the original file. Do not extend byte-preservation claims to edits.

Settings can be exported and imported as a file. This is not cloud synchronization or shared job history. Saved jobs remain local to the Mac.

Nearby-place searches and travel estimates use Apple Maps. Property outlines are drawn by the user, not looked up or verified against parcel records.

## Make the design support the story

Use the listing-folio direction: warm paper, deep green, Newsreader headings, Archivo body copy, real photography, and precise rules and spacing. Keep the native teal house icon. The finished photo leads; the app screenshots explain the work.

Short outcome headings are appropriate. Supporting copy must explain what users can do. Avoid repetitive slogan fragments and unnecessary technical details in the main story. Put deeper instructions in the guides.

The before/after interaction must work with a pointer and keyboard. Keep the finished image and original links usable without JavaScript. Motion should be restrained and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Check before publishing

Does the page speak equally to photographers and agents? Does the customer know the outcome and the next step? Can they find every feature? Are examples genuine and claims verified? Are original files, legacy routes, download links, and the permanent update feed preserved?
